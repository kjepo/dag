    How to use the editor

    Moments

    Each box is a moment: something that happens at one point in time, like “Entries open” or “Judging”. A moment has a date (or a date and a time of day) but no duration. Type its name in the text field and press Enter. Keep typing to add the next one. The field always keeps focus. A moment with its date set gets a thicker border and is placed after the undated moments in its column, sorted by date.

    Split a moment in two: press / (with the text field empty), or click Split… in the properties panel or the ☰ menu, to add a new moment right after the selected moment (the one shown in the panel, or the only box picked). It is only available with exactly one moment selected. You name it. The selected moment keeps the arcs coming in; the moments that came after it now come after the new one.

    Moment properties

    Double-click a moment’s name to rename it right in its box: type, then Enter (or click elsewhere) to keep it, or Esc to cancel.

    Click a box to edit its name, date and notes (a box with notes shows a small note icon; hover over the name to read them), see and remove its arcs (including ones that aren’t drawn), or delete it. The × next to the date clears it. A missing date is suggested (in purple italics) from the connected moments: as soon as the moments before it allow, or else as late as the moments after it allow. Click Use suggested date to keep it. Auto-schedule… in the ☰ menu does that for every moment at once: each moment without a date comes as soon as the moments before it allow (moments with nothing before them get a date you pick). Dates you have set are never moved, and locked moments are left alone. Changes are applied as you make them; Nothing comes before it / Nothing comes after it hide the box’s left / right circle (top / bottom when vertical), for a first or last moment like “Project kickoff” or “Release”: no arcs can then be connected on that side. They can only be ticked while there are no arcs on that side. Save moment (or Enter) confirms and closes the panel. Close with Esc or by clicking the background.

    Deleting: Delete (or Backspace, while nothing is being typed) deletes the moment shown in the panel, or all picked boxes (see Phases; the bar at the bottom also has a Delete button). A folded phase’s box deletes every moment in it. When arcs run through what is deleted, you choose: Delete with arcs, or Delete and reconnect, which links every moment before the deleted ones straight to every moment after them, with the longest total wait along the old way, so nothing may come earlier than before. Undo brings it all back.

    Dependencies

    Every box has a connector on each side. Click one: it turns black, and the connectors on the opposite side of the other boxes turn green. Click a green one to draw the arc. Or press < (with the text field empty) to connect the two newest moments: type “Entries open”, Enter, “Entries close”, Enter, < and “Entries open” comes before “Entries close”. Or drag from a connector and drop the arc anywhere on another box; the box turns green when the arc can go there.

    • An arc always runs from a box’s right connector to another box’s left connector, meaning “the left moment comes before the right one”.
    • Dimmed connectors can’t be used: that arc would create a cycle or already exists.
    • Cancel with Esc, by clicking empty canvas, or by clicking the black connector again.
    • Click an arc to set a wait on it or to remove it.
    • An arc already implied by a longer path (A → B → C makes A → C redundant) is kept but not drawn.

    Date conflicts

    A moment gets a red border when its date doesn’t work: it is earlier than a moment that should come before it (the arc turns red too). Hover over the box to see why. Without a wait, a later moment may be on the same day (in hours, at the same minute) as the one before it.

    Waits. Some moments need time between them: results come a week after judging. Click an arc to give it a wait, e.g. 7 (days) or 4h; the arc shows it as +1w or +4h, and the moment after it may only come once the wait is over. Wait between moments… in the ☰ menu sets a wait for every arc that has none of its own (none by default); 1 day, say, so that each moment is at least the day after the one before. Conflicts, suggestions, slack, Auto-schedule and Push later all count the waits.

    When an edit makes later moments too early (say a moment moves a few days later), a message offers Push later: each of those moments moves later by just enough, and so do the moments after it. A moment with slack absorbs the delay, so the move stops there. Resolve conflicts in the ☰ menu does the same at any time. A locked moment (tick Locked in its properties; the box shows a 🔒) is never moved: if the push reaches it, it stays red, as a sign that something before it has to be earlier. Undo takes a push back.

    Critical path

    Press ! (or use Critical path in the ☰ menu) to show which moments set the date of the last moment. They and the arcs between them turn orange: any delay there delays what comes after. Every other moment gets a small label with its slack, how much later it could be without delaying anything, e.g. +1h 15m or +3d. The properties panel says how late it can be. A moment with a − label is behind: something after it would have to move. A moment without a date counts as early as the moments before it allow; one with no dated moment before it is left out. Press ! again to hide it.

    Timeline

    Timeline in the ☰ menu lists the graph in date order, as it is shown: each moment with its name and notes, and each phase with its name and its first to last date. A folded phase is one line; an open phase has its line, and its moments follow. Moments without a date are listed at the end. Click a moment to open it. Close with ×, Esc, or a click outside.

    Phases

    Any moments can be grouped into a phase, like “Judging”, shown as a single box. Pick the boxes: Shift-click them (or ⌘/Ctrl-click) with a mouse, or on a touch screen press and hold one box, then tap the others. Picked boxes get a blue outline; click or tap one again to unpick it. A bar at the bottom shows how many are picked: Group to phase makes the phase and lets you type its name right in the box, Cancel (or Esc, or a click on the background) unpicks them all. A moment that lies on a path between two picked moments goes into the phase too, so a phase never has arcs going out and coming back in; the same happens when a later arc puts a moment between two of its moments. The box looks like a stack of cards. A badge on top says how many moments it holds; the box shows its start and end: the earliest and latest date inside. It turns orange or red when a moment inside is critical or in conflict. Connect arcs to it as to any box: an arc to it goes to each of its first moments, an arc from it leaves from each of its last ones, so the whole phase comes after (or before) the other moment.

    Click a phase’s box to open it: its moments come out and stay together inside a dashed frame with the phase’s name on top, so you can see which moments belong to it and edit them. Click ▾ before the name to fold them back into one box. Double-click the name, on the frame or on the folded box, to rename the phase right there, as with a moment’s name: type, then Enter (or click elsewhere) to keep it, or Esc to cancel. The × after the name removes the phase and keeps its moments. To add a moment to an open phase, drag it onto the frame (pull it sideways first if it has arcs; the frame turns green), and drop it. Drag a moment out of its frame and drop it outside to take it out of the phase. A folded phase’s box can be dragged the same way, taking all its moments along, and slid up or down (left or right, when vertical) to reorder it among the boxes next to it. A moment that lies between two others of the phase has to stay in it. Phases, open or folded, are saved with the graph.

    Phases can be inside other phases. Open a phase and make a phase of some of its moments, or make a bigger phase around a phase by picking its folded box along with other boxes. A phase must hold all of another phase or none of it. Folding a phase hides everything inside it; opening it shows the phases inside as you left them, folded or open, each in its own frame.

    Days or hours

    A graph is planned in whole days unless Hours and minutes is ticked in the ☰ menu; the choice is saved with the graph. In hours, each moment has a time of day too. Waits are written like 45m, 2h 30m, 1d 4h or 1.5 (hours). When the graph covers more than one date, the boxes show the date under each time.

    Switching from days to hours keeps everything: a moment on Nov 14 becomes Nov 14 00:00. Switching back drops the times of day, after asking.

    A graph saved by the task editor opens too: each task becomes a moment at its start date (or its end date when it has no start).

    Moving around

    • Drag the background, or scroll, to pan. On a touch screen, drag with one finger and pinch with two to zoom.
    • A box with no arcs can be dragged anywhere that isn’t on top of another box. It stays where you put it until it gets an arc; then the automatic layout places it again.
    • A box with arcs can be dragged up or down its column (left or right along its row, when vertical). Drop it past a neighbour to change the order of that column; the layout keeps your order and works around it. Pull it sideways into another column (row) and drop it there to move it to that column, next to the boxes you dropped it between: a box normally sits as far left (up) as its arcs allow, but may go later, as long as it stays after the boxes that lead to it and before the ones it leads to. Reset box order in the ☰ menu goes back to the automatic order and columns.
    • ⌘/Ctrl + scroll, or pinch, to zoom at the pointer. Or use − and +.
    • ↔ Horizontal / ↕ Vertical switches the layout between left-to-right and top-to-bottom. In the vertical layout the connectors are at the top (incoming) and bottom (outgoing) of each box. The choice is saved with the graph.
    • Fit (between − and +) zooms in as far as possible while still showing the whole graph. A graph you open starts out that way too.
    • Show legend (in the ☰ menu) shows the weeks or months each column (or row, when vertical) covers. It is off by default; the choice is remembered in this browser.

    Saving

    • Undo (⌘/Ctrl+Z) and Redo (⇧⌘+Z or Ctrl+Y), also in the ☰ menu, step back and forth through every change since the page was opened, including New and Open. While you are typing in a field, those keys undo the typing instead.
    • The graph is saved in this browser automatically after every change.
    • In the ☰ menu: New starts over and asks for the new graph’s name. Save (⌘/Ctrl+S) saves it as a file under that name without asking. Save as… (⇧⌘/Ctrl+S) asks for a name first, and so does Save when the graph has none yet. Open… (⌘/Ctrl+O) loads a file. The name is shown in the browser tab.
    • In Chrome and Edge you also choose the folder: the first Save of a new graph shows the save dialog once, and after that Save writes over the same file (or the file you opened). Other browsers put each save in Downloads.
    • Share link copies a link that contains the whole graph. Whoever opens it sees a snapshot of the graph as it is now; later edits need a new link. The graph is in the part after #, which is never sent to the server.
